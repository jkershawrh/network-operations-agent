You did more than replay the quickstart. You extended an evidence architecture, introduced a new read-only MCP diagnostic, authored a third incident, tested failure behavior, and produced reviewable proof.

The reusable pattern is intentionally industry portable:

* Current tool observations remain separate from historical context.
* Models may explain evidence but do not create authorization.
* Missing or conflicting required evidence causes abstention.
* Human approval and `action_executed=false` remain invariant.
* Qualification output makes reliability claims repeatable.

Your files are in `$HOME/network-agent-evidence`. They are the “leave with something” outcome for this lab.

== What you learned

You can now explain why an agentic workflow needs more than a model and a collection of tools. Trust comes from the complete system: narrow tool contracts, current evidence with provenance, versioned context, deterministic policy, explicit abstention, platform isolation, and human authority.

You also practiced a repeatable development sequence:

. Establish known-good behavior.
. Trace component and trust boundaries.
. Extend one bounded evidence domain.
. Reject unauthorized input.
. Inject failures and predict behavior.
. Qualify the release against explicit expectations.
. Communicate a supported recommendation without overstating it.

== Claims this lab does not make

The lab does not demonstrate autonomous incident resolution, a measured reduction in mean time to resolution, fewer truck rolls, production capacity, or access to a real telecommunications network. The optional model can improve readability, but the evidence and safety behavior do not depend on model fluency.

== Next steps

Use the exported scenario and brief as a starting point for a design review. When adapting the pattern, define your own approved scopes, source revisions, failure semantics, and authorization boundary before connecting live systems. Add mutation only through a separately reviewed human-approval workflow with platform-enforced permissions—never by widening the diagnostic tool contract.
